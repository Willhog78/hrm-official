# G7 — General Action and Communication

## Goal

Allow useful behavior to be composed, learned, signaled, and imitated from general primitives acting on the same live world used by ecology and human biology.

## Primitive vocabulary

move, inspect, grasp, release, carry, consume, transfer, combine, separate, apply_force, arrange, signal.

These are causal verbs, not technologies or milestones.

## Rule

The causal engine may store primitive sequences and their experienced value. It must not contain named techniques such as farming, spear-making, shelter-building, cooking, professions, or civilization stages.

Primitive actions that manipulate material must move existing material between owned causal pools. They may not create material, create a named object by shortcut, or grant a benefit merely because a sequence is recognized.

Physical benefit must come from the resulting world state. For example, an arrangement may change exposure only because physically arranged material is present; there is no causal "shelter" flag or "build shelter" action.

Manipulation is bounded by carrying mass and bodily effort cost.

## Communication

An agent may signal a bounded primitive sequence to a nearby recognized agent. The receiver may retain and imitate it. Transmission does not guarantee usefulness; reward must come from executing the sequence in the environment.

## Exit condition

At least one useful primitive sequence must be acquired against live world material, change conserved physical state, improve the executing agent's experienced outcome enough to exceed its action cost, and be transmitted to another agent which reproduces the effect by imitation. No named technology may exist in causal state.
